Pre-PT / OT: Preparing for a Career in Physical & Occupational Therapy

A look at what the Pre-PT/OT path involves — and the student leading it within Progress Over Perfection.

What is Pre-PT/OT?

Pre-PT and Pre-OT are pathways for students working toward a Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT) or Doctor of Occupational Therapy (OTD) degree after undergrad. Both are hands-on, patient-facing careers focused on helping people recover movement, function, and independence — whether after an injury, surgery, or a chronic condition.

The road there typically includes prerequisite science coursework, direct observation or shadowing hours across different clinical settings, and standardized testing such as the GRE, which some programs still require. Just like the pre-med or pre-dental tracks, it comes with its own version of the pressure to have everything figured out — which is exactly why this track has a place inside P.O.P.

Physical Therapy (DPT)

Focuses on movement, rehabilitation, and physical function — working in settings like hospitals, outpatient clinics, home health, or sports medicine.

Occupational Therapy (OTD)

Focuses on helping people regain the skills needed for daily living and work, often blending physical, cognitive, and emotional support.

Pre-PT Chair

Joshua Jones

Philadelphia, PA

Senior Exercise Science Pre-PT Track Interested in Home Health & Travel PT

Joshua is a senior majoring in Exercise Science on the Pre-Physical Therapy track. He hopes to specialize in home health or travel physical therapy in the future. To prepare for his career, Joshua has been shadowing physical therapists in a variety of settings, including inpatient hospitals and outpatient clinics, to gain a better understanding of what physical therapy looks like across different environments. He also studied for and took the GRE during the summer before his senior year, as the exam is required by some physical therapy programs. Joshua joined Progress Over Perfection (P.O.P.) because he believes college students still do not prioritize their mental health as much as they should. Through P.O.P., he hopes not only to spread awareness about the importance of mental health but also to inspire students to take practical steps toward improving their mental well-being, especially during stressful times. Ultimately, Joshua hopes students will become role models when it comes to mental health, inspiring their peers, families, and even their future patients, when applicable to their careers, to recognize and value the importance of their mental well-being.
