Pre-Med: Preparing for a Career in Medicine

A look at what the Pre-Med path involves — and the student leading it within Progress Over Perfection.

What is Pre-Med?

Pre-Med is the pathway for students working toward medical school and, eventually, a career as a physician (MD or DO). It’s one of the most academically demanding routes in the pre-health space — blending rigorous science coursework with the MCAT, clinical shadowing, research, and volunteer hours, all while maintaining a competitive GPA.

The pressure to do it all perfectly is real, which is exactly why this track has a place inside P.O.P. Success on the Pre-Med path isn’t about never struggling — it’s about building sustainable habits that let you show up for the long haul.

Coursework & MCAT

A demanding sequence of science prerequisites paired with MCAT preparation, all while protecting a competitive GPA.

Shadowing & Volunteering

Hands-on clinical exposure and community service that build the experience medical schools look for — and the perspective future physicians need.

Pre-Med Chair

Janine Ayeza Escamis

Myrtle Beach, South Carolina

Junior Neuroscience Major Spanish Minor Aspiring Neurologist

Following the Pre-Medicine pathway, she aspires to become a neurologist. She recognizes that the pre-medical curriculum is academically intense, requiring students to balance rigorous coursework, maintain a high GPA, prepare for the MCAT, and secure shadowing and volunteer hours. However, Janine believes that academic rigor does not have to mean burnout. She has learned to accept that not every exam will go perfectly and that a deep understanding of the material is more important than memorizing it, even if it takes additional time. To manage her course load and stress, Janine divides her day into two to three parts, with her mornings and afternoons focused on classes or studying depending on her class schedule, while reserving her evenings for rest. She stops studying at 9:00 PM so that she can call her family or watch a show, giving herself time to reflect and unwind from the day. Additionally, she creates a structured schedule that includes non-negotiable study blocks, meal times, and other daily activities to stay organized, avoid forgetting important tasks, and reduce the stress of meeting deadlines. Most importantly, Janine encourages fellow pre-health students to remember that they are not alone; there are many students navigating the same journey and numerous resources available on campus, including this very organization.
